Volume 1: Executive Summary (Oct '24 - Oct '25)

Over twelve months, I juggled full-time studies, a collapsing relationship, a housing dispute that escalated to a default judgment, my father's medical crises, and the stubborn launch of an AI project. The year began with a strained Christmas, slid into a January separation and lock change, and barreled into April's psychiatric admission and next-day arrest of my former partner. By June, I was defending against an outsized tenancy claim; by July, I was racing deadlines to set it aside. Amid the turbulence, I advanced my Biomedical Science studies at UWA, sat multiple exams, maintained income through hospitality work, and continued building a supplement-interaction app, SuppTracker.

V1 Prologue: Why I Kept Swinging

"I didn't fight because I'm brave. I fought because I hate bad math. A $27k default judgment built on vapor is bad math. If I rolled over, I'd be bankrolling someone else's fiction and teaching the universe that I'm negotiable. I'm not. Same with PHYL2002. When life ran on vibes and threats, ion channels still opened when they were told. In a year where meaning buckled, physiology was one room with gravity."

V1: The Slow Unraveling & Family Crisis

          
The year began with a relationship already volatile. The Christmas trip to Geraldton crystallized this, with Alana spending ~16 hours a day on X (Twitter), contributing nothing, and disconnecting from reality. This detachment continued into January with mythic identities and messiah narratives.

          
By mid-January, it became a safety issue. After discovering she’d picked my bedroom lock, I installed a new lock and a camera. I wasn’t optimizing for harmony anymore; I was optimizing for not getting robbed in my sleep.

          
The ICU Shock

          
The day after a major confrontation, my father was rushed to ICU with sepsis. He spent a week on life support, elected palliative care, then stabilized. Alana showed zero empathy, and the relationship stopped being a support system and became a risk surface.

          
Crisis Points

          
On April 2, she was involuntarily admitted to a psychiatric ward—relief with a guilt tax. I provided clinicians with a detailed timeline. Hours after her April 22 discharge, she was arrested on a warrant and spent the night in the Northbridge Watch House.

        


        

          
V1: Trial by Paperwork

          
As the relationship unraveled, so did housing. Tensions escalated in early 2025. Recognizing the danger, I documented everything: photos, emails, dates. When talking failed, dates didn’t.

          
The $27k Absent Judgment

          
The landlords filed for ~$13,000. On the June 18 hearing date, a bank outage stranded me; the court entered a default judgment for $27,265.62 in my absence.

          
I refused to fold. With 21 days to fight, I standardized my service address, compiled evidence, and filed a Form 16 affidavit on July 9: non-willful absence; no service of the inflated claim; exaggerated, invoice-free charges; and the landlords’ own RTA breach (s79) disposing of goods.

          
The Hearing

          
At the September set-aside hearing the magistrate stayed the judgment and adjourned for the landlords to produce actual invoices—the payload behind their scaffolding. In October I lodged summonses to compel production.

        


        

          
V1: The Anchor of Gravity

          
Biomedical Science at UWA was both pressure and anchor. When life ran on vibes and threats, ion channels still opened when told. I juggled PHYL1001, PHYL2002, and PSYC1101—studying for a February exam while my father was in ICU, writing labs in May while building legal evidence, and sitting finals days after the $27k hit.

          
Living the Textbook

          
Studying systems failing in real time was surreal. Slide: “chronic stress impairs executive function.” My notes: “no kidding.” Physiology became the counter-argument to chaos.

        


        

          
V1: Project: SuppTracker

          
I kept coding an AI-driven supplement-interaction app: FastAPI, React, Docker. It was my project baby—a place where logic held when nothing else did.

          
The Facepalm That Helped

          
The app “worked,” but returned nothing. In July I found the stupid/holy bug: the database was empty. Metaphor unlocked—stop polishing the frame and load the content. Same pattern as the legal case: nice scaffolding, no payload.

          
The Ethical Scaffolding

          
Health tech can’t “move fast and break things.” Every entry needs citations; every output needs context. The most important feature is guardrails.

Volume 1: The Crisis (Oct '24 - Oct '25)


        
        

          
Geraldton Christmas Trip — Coastal Sun, Endless Scroll

          
The house smelled like salt and supermarket bleach. Sunlight came in hard and flat, bleaching thoughts and good intentions. The esky had ice; the plan had optimism. Within 24 hours, the optimism was compost.

          
Alana set up camp on the couch and mainlined X for sixteen hours a day like it was respiratory support. No food brought, no presents, no eye contact with reality. I did the run to the bottle-o, the servo, the bin that always overflows by Boxing Day. She posted threads about star maps and sacred bloodlines; I posted nothing and took the bins out.

        


        


        
        

          
Part One · Chapter 3: The Week That Broke the Timeline (ICU Vigil)

          
The room. Chlorhexidine and lemon wipes over stale air. Fluorescents buzzing at a pitch you only hear when you haven’t slept. Pulse-ox beeps doing metronome duty, speeding up when the nurse rolled him, easing when sedation took. Ventilator’s steady sigh: in–pause–out–pause. A whiteboard in dry-erase hieroglyphs: names, pain scores, “NIL BY MOUTH.”

          
Studying between visits. Flashcards on phone because paper curls under sanitizer. Ten-minute blocks: HPA axis (CRH → ACTH → cortisol); sympathetic vs parasympathetic bullets; back to count ventilator cycles like reps. Voice memo: “Cortisol peaks ~8 a.m.; mine is 24/7—short-term-memory = trash.” Bench near the lifts: annotated slide—“this is why you can’t decide what to eat”—underlined twice. Alarms titled “Food Now, Idiot.”

        


        


        
        

          
April 2 — Psychiatric Admission Day (Relief with a Guilt Tax)

          
Triage room: hope posters, a clock that didn’t care. She was in messiah mode; I was in logistics. The nurse used the soft voice; Alana used prophecy. Questions landed like paper airplanes.

          
I handed clinicians a bullet-point timeline: dates, behaviours, sleep, phone usage, detachment from reality. “This isn’t malice; it’s a very loud signal.” When they made it involuntary, her stare said traitor. Maybe I was—defecting to gravity. I paid the guilt tax at the vending machine with coins I didn’t have. Text to family: She’s safe and angry; both are data. Home was finally quiet—earned and awful.

        


        


        
        

          
June 18 — The Bank Outage (How $13k Became $27,265.62)

          
Night before: UBank said “service unavailable.” Phone, web, sacrifice—same error. 7 a.m.: still dead. Fuel light glaring. Tried to line up a ride; got the shrug emoji in real life.

          
I called the registry, left a message, emailed screenshots: “Attempting to attend.” Watched the window narrow like a pupil. There’s a moment you stop trying for a miracle and start making a record. I made the record.

          
11:40 a.m.: “Matter has been heard.” Thirty minutes later: $27,265.62. Chest: hollow heat. Took a photo of my face, then the screen, then the fuel gauge like I was building an alibi. Opened a fresh doc titled Counter-Offensive and made a checklist, because when you can’t move, you plan.

        


        


        
        

          
Part Two · Chapter 7: From $13k to $27k (Documentation, Perfect Storm, Counter-Offensive)

          
Research map (triage). Residential Tenancies Act 1987 (WA): s 76A (condition reports), s 79 (disposal of goods), plus compensation, depreciation, mitigation. Magistrates Court practice directions for set-asides. I built two columns: “Elements they must prove → Evidence we actually have” versus “Defences → Exhibits.” Every line got a source, screenshot, or file path.

          
Evidence bundles (forensic admin). Physical binder with tabs, mirror on USB, index page mapping exhibit codes. Panic converted into pagination.

          
September hearing beats. Posture vs payload: Magistrate—“Invoices?” Agent—“Quotes.” Magistrate—“Different thing.” ... The stay: “Judgment stayed; matter adjourned. Produce the invoices.” Muscles I’d flexed since June finally let go.

        


        

        
        
        

          
July — SuppTracker Debugging (Scaffolding Is Not a Feature)

          
Symptoms: app runs, UI clean, endpoints cheerful, results… nothing. The kind of nothing that pretends to be minimalism. Trace: frontend → API → service layer → DB query. Logs: “all good.” DB: “what DB?” Connection string pointed to a pristine, unseeded instance. Of course.

          
Fix: seed script, point env to the real database, add a health check that counts rows and screams if the number is suspiciously zen. Dev banner: CONTENT: 0 ROWS — DO BETTER. Reload. “Magnesium + SSRI” returns a properly dull interaction summary with citations. I wanted to hug Postgres.

          
README addition: Scaffolding is not a feature. Load the content. I taped that above the desk. It also described the lawsuit.

        


        

        
        
        

          
Part Three · Chapter 8: Physiology as Counter-Argument (Studying HPA While Living It)

          
Lecture moments that landed. HPA cascade slide (CRH → ACTH → cortisol). Lecturer: “Elegant until it isn’t.” I wrote: mine isn’t. Circadian cortisol graph: neat morning peak, graceful taper; mine looked like a picket fence. “Chronic cortisol impairs hippocampal memory”—underlined, then: this is why I can recite statutes but forget breakfast.

          
Thesis of the year. Name → map → nudge. Treat self like a system with bottlenecks: sleep debt, nutrition, stress load. “Elegant until it isn’t” became my cue to pause and simplify.

        


        


        
        

          
October — VAD Preparations (Procedure as Love)

          
He picked a date and the world sorted itself: music, visitors, forms, the shirt he wanted (“the decent one, not the wedding one”), the joke someone had to tell so the room didn’t calcify. We built a playlist that didn’t beg for tears—his road songs that smelled like diesel and sunburn. I packed lip balm and a charger. Asked who he wanted in the room and who he wanted loved from the hallway. “Keep it small; make it true.”

          
The nurse walked us through each step with the kind of care that forgives the universe for a minute. He signed; we witnessed; the pen felt too light for the weight of it. ... On the day, we treated procedure like liturgy. He said what he needed to say without drama; we did the same without bargaining. When it went quiet, it wasn’t empty. I stepped outside into a rude, perfect blue. Hated it. Needed it. Went home and updated the timeline because love deserves accuracy.

        

        
        
        
Volume 2: The Reconstruction (Nov '25 —)

        
        
        

          
November '25 — The Quiet Admin

          
There is a silence after sustained crisis that feels less like peace and more like an ambush you haven't found yet. For the first week of November, I kept checking my email for summonses, my phone for family emergencies. The quiet was loud. My HPA axis, so used to a "continuous infusion," didn't know how to taper. I had to give it a new job, or it would invent one.

          
So I gave it a job. I opened PHYL2002. I opened the tenancy file. I opened the SuppTracker repo. And I started doing the admin. Not frantic, panic-driven admin. Just... quiet admin. Converting panic into pagination, but this time without the panic. Treating my study notes with the same procedural rigor as a court document. Treating the SuppTracker database with the same reverence as my father's VAD procedure.

          
The new feeling: It's not "motivation." It's structure. It's the "Forensic Admin" protocol applied to reconstruction. The V1 lesson was "I'm not heroic. I'm stubborn with receipts." The V2 application is to be stubborn with receipts before the emergency. It's building the scaffolding and loading the payload, on my own terms, in the quiet.
